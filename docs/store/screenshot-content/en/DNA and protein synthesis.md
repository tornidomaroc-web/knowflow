# DNA and protein synthesis

## Where genetic information is kept
Genetic information is stored in the nucleus, carried by DNA. A DNA molecule is made of two complementary strands wound into a double helix, and its building block is the nucleotide.

## DNA replication
Before a cell divides, its DNA is copied during the S phase of interphase. The two strands separate, and each one serves as a template for a new strand. Replication is called **semi-conservative**: each new molecule keeps one original strand and one new strand.

## From gene to protein
Genetic information is expressed by building proteins, in two steps:
1. **Transcription** happens in the nucleus. A gene is copied into messenger RNA.
2. **Translation** happens in the cytoplasm, on ribosomes. The messenger RNA is read codon by codon, each codon being three bases, and amino acids are joined one after another.

## Features of the genetic code
- **Universal:** almost the same in every living thing.
- **Redundant:** several codons can stand for the same amino acid.
- **Non-overlapping:** codons are read one after another.
- **Start and stop codons** mark where translation begins and ends.

## Mutations
A mutation is a change in the sequence of a gene. It can change the amino acid that is added, and so the protein, and a new trait may appear as a result.
