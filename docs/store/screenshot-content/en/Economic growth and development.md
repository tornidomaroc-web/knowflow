# Economic growth and development

## What economic growth is
Economic growth is a lasting rise in the amount a country produces over a long period. It is usually measured by the yearly change in gross domestic product (GDP).

## What drives growth
- **Labour:** the number of working people and how well they are trained.
- **Capital:** machines, equipment and the infrastructure that is invested in.
- **Technical progress:** new ways of producing that make labour and capital more productive.

## From growth to development
Growth is a change in quantity; development is a change in quality that reaches people's lives. Output can rise while health, education and living conditions stay the same: growth without development.

## Measuring development
The Human Development Index combines three dimensions:
1. **Health**, measured by life expectancy at birth.
2. **Education**, measured by mean and expected years of schooling.
3. **Standard of living**, measured by gross national income per person.

## What holds development back
- Too little investment in people's skills and health.
- Social and regional inequality.
- Dependence on exporting raw materials.

## Summary
Growth is necessary for development, but it is not enough. Development needs the gains of growth to turn into real improvements in people's lives.
