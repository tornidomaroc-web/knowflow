# The First World War (1914 to 1918)

## Long-term causes
- **Imperial rivalry:** European powers competed for colonies, markets and raw materials.
- **The alliance system:** Europe was split into two camps, the Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy) and the Triple Entente (France, Britain and Russia).
- **The arms race:** countries built up their armies and navies in preparation for war.
- **Nationalism:** national demands grew stronger, especially in the Balkans.

## The trigger
The heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne was assassinated in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914. Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia, and the allied powers joined one after another.

## The phases of the war
1. **War of movement (1914):** fast offensives that stopped at the Battle of the Marne.
2. **Trench warfare (1915 to 1917):** frozen fronts and long, costly battles such as Verdun.
3. **The end (1917 to 1918):** Russia withdrew, the United States joined, and the armistice was signed on 11 November 1918.

## Consequences
- **Human:** millions killed and wounded.
- **Economic:** widespread destruction and heavy debts for European countries.
- **Political:** the Treaty of Versailles in 1919, new states in Europe, and the creation of the League of Nations.

## Summary
The First World War redrew the map of Europe, weakened its place in the world, and left causes for later conflicts.
