# Cellular respiration and fermentation

## Why cells need it
Every cell needs energy to work: to contract a muscle, to move substances across its membrane, to build new molecules. It takes that energy from organic matter, mainly glucose, and stores it in a small molecule called ATP.

## The three stages of respiration
1. **Glycolysis** happens in the cytoplasm and needs no oxygen. One glucose molecule is split into two molecules of pyruvate, with a net gain of two ATP.
2. **The Krebs cycle** happens in the matrix of the mitochondria. Pyruvate is broken down completely, carbon dioxide is released, and electron carriers are loaded.
3. **The respiratory chain** sits in the inner membrane of the mitochondria. It passes electrons to oxygen, the final acceptor, which forms water and produces most of the ATP.

## The energy yield
Breaking down one glucose molecule completely gives between 36 and 38 ATP. Respiration is therefore a high-yield way to release energy.

## Fermentation
Without oxygen, some cells turn to fermentation, which happens entirely in the cytoplasm:
- **Alcoholic fermentation**, in yeast, produces ethanol and carbon dioxide.
- **Lactic fermentation**, in muscle cells during intense effort, produces lactic acid.

Glucose is only partly broken down, so fermentation yields just two ATP per glucose molecule.

## Summary
| | Respiration | Fermentation |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | Needed | Not needed |
| Where | Cytoplasm and mitochondria | Cytoplasm only |
| Glucose breakdown | Complete | Partial |
| ATP per glucose | 36 to 38 | 2 |
